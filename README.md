Introduction



The Framingham Heart Study is a long-running cohort study focused on cardiovascular health in residents of Framingham, Massachusetts. It has played an important role in the development of cardiovascular epidemiology, particularly by helping establish the idea of cardiovascular risk factors and the combined influence of multiple risk factors. The study was initiated in 1948 with an original cohort of 5,209 participants. After enrollment, participants underwent examinations approximately every two years and were continuously monitored for the occurrence of cardiovascular events.



The dataset used here represents a subset of the information collected within the Framingham Heart Study. It contains laboratory measurements, clinical information, questionnaire responses, and event-related data for 4,434 individuals. Clinical measurements were obtained during three examination periods separated by approximately six years, covering roughly the years 1956 to 1968. Participants were then followed for 24 years to determine whether they experienced outcomes such as angina pectoris, myocardial infarction, cardiovascular disease, stroke, hypertension, or death.



For this assignment, only measurements from the first examination period are considered, together with outcomes observed during the subsequent 24-year follow-up. Each outcome is treated as a binary variable indicating whether the event occurred at any point during this period. The exact timing of the event is therefore not taken into account.



The dataset was processed using procedures intended to preserve participant anonymity and confidentiality. As a result, it should not be regarded as suitable for publication or for analyses requiring the original identifiable study data.



In this project, the aim is to estimate the causal effect of cigarette smoking (CURSMOKE) on the risk of Hypertension (HYPERTEN).
